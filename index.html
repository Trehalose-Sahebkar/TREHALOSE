The science

Why trehalose?

A naturally occurring disaccharide with an unusual molecular architecture and a strikingly broad therapeutic profile.

Trehalose, also known as mycose, is a non-reducing disaccharide of two glucose units joined by a stable α,α-1,1-glycosidic bond. This unusual linkage gives the molecule exceptional chemical stability and chaperone-like activity, allowing it to stabilise proteins and cell membranes under stress.

Its most studied property is the induction of autophagy, the cell's housekeeping pathway for clearing misfolded proteins and damaged organelles. Because impaired autophagy contributes to neurodegeneration, atherosclerosis, metabolic disease and ageing, trehalose has become an attractive and well-tolerated candidate for therapeutic intervention.

Our programme spans the full translational arc, from mechanistic and preclinical work to randomised, placebo-controlled trials in patients, with the aim of establishing where, and for whom, trehalose delivers genuine clinical benefit.

♻

Autophagy induction

Enhances clearance of toxic protein aggregates through mTOR-independent pathways.

❖

Protein stabilisation

Chaperone-like activity that prevents aberrant folding of vulnerable proteins.

★

Antioxidant defence

Strengthens endogenous defences and reduces damaging oxidative stress.

▿

Anti-inflammatory action

Suppresses pro-inflammatory signalling and inflammasome activation.

Clinical trials

Translating mechanism into patient outcomes

Registered, prospectively designed trials evaluating trehalose across several indications.

Therapeutic effects of trehalose in patients with Alzheimer's disease (MASHIANE)

A pilot randomised, triple-blind, placebo-controlled trial in patients with probable Alzheimer's disease, evaluating intravenous trehalose against normal saline over 12 weeks. Cognitive outcomes are assessed by MMSE and Clinical Dementia Rating.

Design: Randomised, triple-blind, placebo-controlled Intervention: Trehalose 15 g/week IV, 12 weeks Registration: IRCT20130829014521N15

Oral trehalose in type 2 diabetes: glycaemia, inflammation and quality of life

A pilot randomised controlled trial examining whether oral trehalose improves glycaemic control, reduces inflammatory burden and affects quality of life in patients with type 2 diabetes.

Design: Pilot randomised controlled trial Route: Oral Status: Published

Trehalose and mortality in intensive care unit patients

A triple-blind, randomised, placebo-controlled superiority trial enrolling 200 ICU-admitted patients at Imam Reza Hospital, Mashhad, testing whether trehalose reduces organ failure and improves survival.

Design: Triple-blind, randomised, placebo-controlled Intervention: 100 mL of 15% trehalose IV vs normal saline Sample size: 200 patients

Trehalose in traumatic head injury: inflammation, oxidative stress and mortality

A double-blind, placebo-controlled randomised trial in patients with head trauma, assessing inflammatory markers, oxidative stress, clinical symptoms and mortality.

Design: Double-blind, placebo-controlled, randomised Setting: Intensive care

Trehalose supplementation in pertrochanteric fracture

A randomised controlled trial evaluating the effect of trehalose on bone healing, physical function and pain following pertrochanteric fracture.

Design: Randomised controlled trial Status: Published in Scientific Reports, 2026

Intravenous trehalose in acid sphingomyelinase deficiency (Niemann–Pick A and B)

An open-label, single-arm pilot study of intravenous trehalose in patients with acid sphingomyelinase deficiency, with quality of life, serum biomarkers and high-resolution lung CT assessed before and after treatment.

Design: Open-label, single-arm Intervention: Trehalose 15 g/week IV, 12 weeks Status: Published

Trehalose in lysosomal storage disorders: Niemann–Pick and Sanfilippo syndrome

A phase 2 single-arm trial assessing quality of life and paraclinical findings in patients with Niemann–Pick disease or mucopolysaccharidosis type III, including serum enzyme activity and urinary glycosaminoglycan concentrations.

Design: Single-arm, phase 2 Intervention: Trehalose 15 g/week IV, 12 weeks Registration: IRCT

Trehalose in multiple sulfatase deficiency

A non-randomised interventional study of intravenous trehalose in multiple sulfatase deficiency, a rare inherited disorder with no established treatment.

Design: Non-randomised, single-arm Intervention: Trehalose 15 g/week IV, 12 weeks Setting: Akbar Children's Hospital, Mashhad
